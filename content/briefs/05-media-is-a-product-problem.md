# Editorial brief 05

## Working title

Media Is a Product Problem, Not Just a Content Problem

## Core question

Why do media organizations underperform when they treat content as the product rather than one component of a larger system?

## Thesis

Content is an essential input, but a media product also includes programming, packaging, interfaces, distribution, audience feedback, community relationships, business rules, and the workflows that make all of it repeatable. Treating content as the whole product hides the decisions that determine discovery, return, participation, and economics. Media gets stronger when editorial, product, marketing, and operations share responsibility for the same system.

## Why this matters

AI makes content cheaper to produce, which increases the cost of confusing supply with value. Organizations that respond by publishing more may amplify weak discovery, poor interfaces, fragmented audience relationships, and operational debt. A product model helps teams decide what to build around content rather than merely how to make more of it.

## Audience

Media founders, editorial and content leaders, product managers, marketers, audience and community teams, operators, creators, and investors evaluating media businesses.

## Key arguments

1. A piece of content can be excellent while the surrounding media product remains hard to discover, use, trust, or return to.
2. Programming is a product decision because sequence, cadence, format, and context shape audience behavior.
3. Distribution is a product problem: interfaces and channels determine how value reaches people and how they return.
4. Marketing and positioning help a market understand the category and expected use, especially during format transitions.
5. Audience and community relationships require feedback and participation surfaces beyond publishing.
6. Internal workflows affect the public product because operational friction changes quality, speed, consistency, and recovery.
7. Revenue models are product constraints, not a commercial layer added after editorial decisions.
8. AI increases the need for product clarity because abundant output makes undifferentiated content easier to substitute.

## Practical examples

- Posta: programming, packaging, production, marketing, branded audio, distribution, audience feedback, and regional operations around a new format.
- Roxom TV: the public stream as the visible surface of programming, data, graphics, distributed production, tooling, automation, and recovery.
- LiveOverlay and Broadcast Control Room: public examples of tools that sit around content but materially shape the media product.
- Referí: an interactive media experience where user action and comparison are part of the product, not an article wrapped in an interface.
- Narrative Mechanics: use its models to show how positioning and distribution affect whether a product becomes legible before direct comparison.

## Counterarguments

- Distinctive editorial work can itself be the strongest product advantage and should not be subordinated to feature thinking.
- Product frameworks can encourage shallow engagement optimization or unnecessary software.
- Small creator-led publications may deliberately keep the surrounding system minimal.
- Some distribution and revenue constraints sit outside the publisher's control, limiting what product work can solve.
