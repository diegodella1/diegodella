# Editorial brief 04

## Working title

AI-Mediated Discovery: What Changes When People Stop Browsing

## Core question

How do media, marketing, and public identity change when an AI system retrieves and interprets sources before a person visits them?

## Thesis

When discovery moves from browsing pages to receiving synthesized answers, the first audience is often a retrieval system. That does not eliminate human judgment or websites, but it changes the route: entity clarity, provenance, structure, citations, and consistent relationships become part of distribution. Publishers must build strong origins that machines can parse without flattening the ideas those origins exist to express.

## Why this matters

Search, feeds, social platforms, and AI assistants increasingly mediate what becomes visible. A page may influence an answer without receiving a visit, while weak or inconsistent source identity can cause correct work to be ignored, merged, or misattributed. Marketing, editorial, product, and technical teams need one model for this changed discovery path.

## Audience

Publishers, media and marketing leaders, product teams, researchers, writers, communications teams, and people responsible for public knowledge or reputation.

## Key arguments

1. Browsing will not disappear, but more discovery journeys will begin and sometimes end inside an answer interface.
2. The retrieval system is an intermediary audience with different needs from a human reader.
3. Entity consistency matters because systems must resolve who created a source and how pages relate.
4. Canonicals, structured data, dates, authorship, internal links, feeds, and clear prose are editorial infrastructure—not a replacement for authority.
5. Original evidence and durable external references matter more when summaries can detach claims from presentation.
6. Zero-click or no-click discovery changes measurement: influence may rise while referral traffic does not.
7. Owned origins remain important because they provide the stable source against which intermediaries can be checked.
8. Writing for retrieval should increase precision, not produce keyword stuffing or generic machine prose.

## Practical examples

- Trace a route from a question about Diego to About, a work case, an external proof source, and a consistent Person `@id`.
- Compare a clear case-study paragraph with a vague “intersection of technology and creativity” statement and show what each permits a retrieval system to infer.
- Use Writing for the Filter, Origin Gravity, Occlusion Bias, and The Proxy Self as distinct parts of the argument: mediation, origin, visibility, and identity.
- Use RSS and sitemap updates to show the difference between inventory, freshness, and evidence.
- Use AI assistants as a test environment, while making clear that one answer is not a stable benchmark or ranking signal.

## Counterarguments

- AI answer interfaces may continue to depend heavily on conventional search indexes and browsing behavior.
- Structured clarity cannot compensate for weak authority, thin evidence, or lack of external corroboration.
- Optimizing too aggressively for retrieval can flatten voice and make content less valuable to people.
- Referral and citation behavior varies by product, geography, query, and model and should not be generalized from anecdotes.
