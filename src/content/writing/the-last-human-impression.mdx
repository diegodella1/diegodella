<div class="section-number">Section I / Four</div>
<h2 class="section-title">Three Agents, One Gap</h2>
</div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<div class="main-col">
<p>When an assistant reads a page for someone, the request and the human encounter are different events. A publisher needs to know which one its metrics record before treating traffic as attention.</p>
<p>The word "AI agent" covers too much ground to be useful without a taxonomy. There are at least three distinct types, each with a different relationship to that surface, and the strategic argument changes depending on which one you're dealing with.</p>
<div class="agent-types">
<div class="agent-types-header">Three agent types: distinct relationships to the Impression Gap</div>
<div class="agent-types-grid">
<div class="agent-type">
<div class="agent-type-label">Type I</div>
<div class="agent-type-name">The Assisted Consumer</div>
<div class="agent-type-desc">A person asking an AI to summarize, compare, or explain. The human initiates, receives the output, and retains the experience. The advertising surface is compressed but not eliminated: the human is still present at reception.</div>
<div class="agent-type-gap gap-partial">Gap: partial</div>
</div>
<div class="agent-type">
<div class="agent-type-label">Type II</div>
<div class="agent-type-name">The Background Filter</div>
<div class="agent-type-desc">Software operating without human input: email triage, notification filtering, the invisible layer that decides what never reaches the inbox. The human never sees what was discarded. This type operates at scale today, not in future tense. It discards. It does not select and present. That distinction is why it creates the Gap.</div>
<div class="agent-type-gap gap-real">Gap: real and present</div>
</div>
<div class="agent-type">
<div class="agent-type-label">Type III</div>
<div class="agent-type-name">The Delegated Actor</div>
<div class="agent-type-desc">An agent that not only filters but acts (purchases, books, responds) on behalf of a human who may not review the specifics. Exists in early form for routine tasks. The advertising surface doesn't apply at all: there is no human moment to intercept.</div>
<div class="agent-type-gap gap-forming">Gap: forming</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<p>The urgency lives in Type II, already operating, already compressing the advertising surface in email, search, and filtered feeds. The structural horizon lives in the transition toward Type III. Both are held together by the same concept.</p>
<div class="concept">
<div class="concept-label">Core Concept</div>
<div class="concept-name">The Impression Gap</div>
<div class="concept-def">The structural void created when agent mediation sits between content and direct human experience. Not a targeting failure, not fixable with better data. The Impression Gap is the absence of the surface the current content economy was built to exploit. Its depth varies by agent type and domain. Its direction is consistent: expanding.<br /><br />This concept extends <strong>Agent Capture</strong> from Occlusion Bias, which describes the same transition from the distribution side. The Impression Gap describes it from the economic surface side: what disappears for the content creator when the human is no longer directly present at reception.</div>
</div>
<p>The Gap is not uniform across domains. It opens fastest where tasks are most legible: research, triage, scheduling, comparison shopping. It is shallowest where the experience itself is the point: live events, real-time interaction with verified identity, communities built on genuine mutual recognition. The effect varies by task; the table below separates those cases.</p>
<div class="timeline">
<div class="timeline-header">Where the Impression Gap is open, opening, and bounded</div>
<div class="timeline-row">
<div class="timeline-period">Open now</div>
<div class="timeline-desc"> <strong>Search and informational queries.</strong> AI assistants synthesize rather than route for a growing share of queries. The click-through (and its ad impression) is replaced by a generated answer. <span class="timeline-signal">Signal: zero-click rate growth in Search Console; declining referral traffic from informational queries across major publishers since 2023; Cloudflare data showing non-human crawlers as a majority of some sites' total requests.</span> </div>
</div>
<div class="timeline-row">
<div class="timeline-period">Opening</div>
<div class="timeline-desc"> <strong>Email and notification triage.</strong> AI-assisted inbox management is growing in enterprise and tech-forward segments. The pre-open impression migrates from human to agent for an increasing share of B2B communications. <span class="timeline-signal">Signal: open rate compression in technology-sector B2B email since 2024; growth of AI-powered inbox management tools across enterprise environments.</span> </div>
</div>
<div class="timeline-row">
<div class="timeline-period">Forming</div>
<div class="timeline-desc"> <strong>Research-intensive purchasing.</strong> For complex considered decisions (enterprise software, financial products, high-consideration categories), agent-assisted research is beginning to replace unaided discovery. <span class="timeline-signal">Signal: growth of AI shopping and research assistants; early data on AI-influenced purchase decisions in enterprise software.</span> </div>
</div>
<div class="timeline-row">
<div class="timeline-period">Participation</div>
<div class="timeline-desc"> <strong>High-stakes, high-pleasure, high-identity experiences.</strong> Contexts structurally resistant to full agent mediation: live events, physical presence, communities requiring verified identity, real-time interaction where <em>being there</em> is the product. These are candidates for sustained direct human attention. <span class="timeline-signal">Live events, physical presence, and communities built around participation are the experiences this argument expects to remain most resistant to delegation. In these settings, attending or taking part can be the value someone seeks. That is a hypothesis about where direct human attention may remain important, not a permanent floor I can establish in advance.</span> </div>
</div>
<div class="timeline-note"><strong>Note on adoption speed:</strong> Timing differs by task, audience, and market. This table is a set of scenarios to investigate. It does not establish a fixed regional lag or a deadline for investing. Check what your audience already delegates before applying it.</div>
</div>
</div>
<div class="margin-col">
<div class="margin-note">The Background Filter discards invisibly. Recommendation engines select and present visibly. The Gap requires the former. That distinction matters for where you build.</div>
<p>Agent Capture concerns the distribution route. The Impression Gap concerns the distance between machine processing and a human encounter.</p>

</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="section-meta">
<div class="section-number">Section II / Four</div>
<h2 class="section-title">Reach Is Indexing</h2>
</div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<div class="main-col">
<p>As the Impression Gap widens, reach starts measuring something different from what it was designed to measure. A growing portion of what registers as an impression or feed encounter is agent-mediated: evaluated, in most cases discarded, before a human experiences it. The number moves. The human exposure behind it moves more slowly. The instruments were not built to show the difference.</p>
<p>The concept that replaces reach as the operative logic is <strong>indexing</strong>: being in the reference set an agent draws from when constructing an answer, filtering a feed, or surfacing a recommendation. Being indexed is not the same as being seen. But it is increasingly what being seen means when the first recipient of content is a machine.</p>
<p>Retrieval and model training are different processes. Making an archive accessible can help a retrieval system find relevant pages; it does not ensure that the material enters training data, receives a higher ranking, or gets cited. Selection differs across products. A publisher should examine actual answers and referrals before treating its archive as an acquisition asset.</p>
<p>The strategic argument operates on a different horizon. As AI evaluation architectures mature, the inputs to good indexing converge with the inputs to genuine editorial authority: specificity that survives verification, consistency of perspective across time, positions maintained under pressure. These are not abstract virtues. They are becoming the signals that more capable agent evaluation will learn to weight. Building for that horizon rather than gaming the current state is the recommendation, not a certainty about outcomes, but the bet with the better long-term structure.</p>
<div class="pull-line">Attribution also varies. A system may cite a source, summarize it without a useful route back, or produce an answer without retrieving that page at all. Being available to the system is therefore a different outcome from receiving traffic or revenue. The economic return needs to be tested separately.</div>
<div class="compare">
<div class="compare-col">
<div class="compare-label">Reach logic: what you optimized for</div>
<ul>
<li>Maximize impressions across surfaces</li>
<li>Hook in the first three seconds</li>
<li>Frequency and engagement drive distribution</li>
<li>Volume compounds audience</li>
<li>Success: eyes on content</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="compare-col">
<div class="compare-label accent">Indexing logic: what compounds from here</div>
<ul>
<li class="hi">Be in the reference set for your domain</li>
<li class="hi">Depth and verifiable specificity over hook</li>
<li class="hi">Consistency of perspective over time</li>
<li class="hi">Credibility that survives agent evaluation</li>
<li class="hi">Success: cited or synthesized when it counts</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<p>Keep the archive usable: stable links, clear dates, named authors, primary sources, and corrections readers can find. These help people inspect the work and can make retrieval easier. They do not guarantee that an assistant will select the work or that selection will support the business.</p>
</div>
<div class="margin-col">
<div class="margin-note">Indexing today favors incumbents. The strategic argument is about the direction of travel as evaluation systems mature. Build for the horizon, not the current state.</div>

</div>
</div>

<div class="section-meta">
<div class="section-number">Section III / Four</div>
<h2 class="section-title">Three Models to Test</h2>
</div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<div class="main-col">
<p>The three models below are proposals to test. An established archive or direct audience may give a publisher a starting advantage, but neither determines the outcome. A newer publication can start with a narrower domain and examine whether the work produces repeat attention and a viable return.</p>
<p>The models assume that some people will respond to delegated routine consumption by seeking more deliberate engagement. Others may spend the time elsewhere. Do not assume the size of either group. Look at repeat reading, replies, subscriptions, and attendance in your audience, then ask what those people value enough to support.</p>
<p>Media organizations face an existential version of the Impression Gap: if the human eye isn't present at reception, the advertising surface disappears. For brands, the problem is more ambiguous. An agent that executes a purchase decision the human has already made or delegated is not necessarily a loss. The three models below are survival architecture for media; for brands, Model I is primary and Models II and III are supplementary.</p>
<div class="models">
<div class="model">
<div class="model-number">Model I</div>
<div class="model-name">Authority of Reference</div>
<div class="model-origin">Predates advertising as: specialist publishing, expert reference, domain media</div>
<div class="model-desc">Being the source agents surface and audiences seek for genuine interpretation of a domain. Advertising rents access to attention. Authority of reference builds a position in the judgment architecture of both agents and the humans they serve, on a different time scale, toward a different kind of durability.</div>
<div class="model-why"><strong>Why it compounds:</strong> Consistent perspective plus domain specificity plus time produces source reputation that is slow to build and expensive to replicate. The economic model (licensing, subscriptions, inbound authority) is still forming for AI contexts. Building the archive may help, but the revenue path remains unresolved and should be tested.</div>
<div class="model-caveat">Caveat: benefits incumbents disproportionately. Being synthesized rather than cited means the revenue mechanism is unresolved. The kill criterion for this model is about the asset, not the revenue. See the kill criteria below.</div>
</div>
<div class="model">
<div class="model-number">Model II</div>
<div class="model-name">Direct Human Access</div>
<div class="model-origin">Predates advertising as: subscriptions, events, community, direct correspondence</div>
<div class="model-desc">Spaces where agent mediation is incomplete or structurally excluded. A distinction matters: newsletters and digital communities are partially mediatable: an agent can summarize a newsletter for you. Physical events, real-time interaction with verified identity, communities requiring genuine mutual recognition: structurally resistant to full substitution.</div>
<div class="model-why"><strong>What to test:</strong> People who seek out an event, publication, or community may value the direct experience enough to pay for it. Ask what they value and test an offer with them. A price increase is a hypothesis about that audience, not an automatic result of more routine consumption moving to agents.</div>
<div class="model-caveat">Caveat: distinguish partially-mediatable (newsletters, forums) from non-delegable (live, physical, real-time verified). The business model implications are different; the former competes with agent summarization, the latter does not.</div>
</div>
<div class="model">
<div class="model-number">Model III</div>
<div class="model-name">Consequential Voice</div>
<div class="model-origin">Predates advertising as: named editorial authority, accountability under risk, brand reputation with stakes</div>
<div class="model-desc">The organization or individual that publishes under real identity, takes positions, and has something to lose when wrong. The differentiator is asymmetry: when being wrong costs something visible, being right means something real. That asymmetry is what audiences pay for when the alternative is infinite content with no author who has anything at stake.</div>
<div class="model-why"><strong>What readers can examine:</strong> Who makes the editorial decisions, how challenges are handled, and what changes after an error. These practices can apply to human and AI-assisted work. Their value depends on whether the source’s operators actually take responsibility and whether readers use that record.</div>
<div class="model-caveat">Horizon risk: as AI personas become more persistent and simulated accountability more convincing, the line will blur for some audiences. Specificity and verifiable track record are the defense.</div>
</div>
</div>
<div class="kill-ops">
<div class="kill-ops-header"> <span>Kill Criteria: for people making decisions, not evaluating theses</span> <span>3 signals</span> </div>
<div class="kill-ops-row">
<div class="kill-ops-signal">Asset not compounding after 12 months</div>
<div class="kill-ops-action"><strong>Signal:</strong> your domain-specific body of work has not grown in depth, specificity, or consistency over a 12-month period. Measure this concretely: the number of pieces that take a falsifiable position, the percentage that cite primary sources or original data, the frequency of publication under your name in a single domain. <strong>Threshold:</strong> if those three numbers are flat or declining, you are producing content volume without building an indexing asset. <strong>Action:</strong> do not diagnose this as a distribution or monetization problem. It is an asset problem. Narrow the domain further, increase the specificity of each piece, and hold the publication frequency for 90 days before reassessing. Revenue from authority of reference is a downstream question; the asset has to exist first.</div>
</div>
<div class="kill-ops-row">
<div class="kill-ops-signal">Direct access stagnant vs. reach</div>
<div class="kill-ops-action"><strong>If owned and direct channels</strong> (newsletter subscribers, community members, event attendees) <strong>are not growing as a share of total audience over 12 months,</strong> even as distributed reach holds or grows, you are not converting presence into access. Reach maintained, direct access stagnant is the specific failure mode to act on, not the aggregate number.</div>
</div>
<div class="kill-ops-row">
<div class="kill-ops-signal">Deliberate minority contracting</div>
<div class="kill-ops-action"><strong>If behavioral signals of deliberate engagement</strong> (completion rates, response rates, direct conversion, attendance) <strong>are declining as a share of total audience interactions,</strong> the fraction of your audience that will benefit from the three models is contracting. This is the earliest warning that your domain is moving toward passive consumption. It does not mean abandon the strategy. It means the pricing, positioning, and community structure need adjustment before the gap becomes unbridgeable.</div>
</div>
</div>
</div>
<div class="margin-col">
<div class="margin-note">For media: existential. For brands: a positioning challenge for agent-era purchase criteria. The models are the same; the urgency is not.</div>

<div class="section-meta">
<div class="section-number">Section IV / Four</div>
<h2 class="section-title">The Position</h2>
</div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<div class="main-col">
<p>This paper is for media builders, brand strategists, and editorial directors: anyone whose economic model is directly attached to what happens in the space between something published and the human who receives it. The Impression Gap closes that space in a growing number of contexts. The three models are the structures that survive and strengthen as it does.</p>
<p>The examples describe different forms of mediation. Their scale and economic effects need evidence from each setting. The three proposed responses should be judged by what they do for a particular audience, not by agreement with the forecast.</p>
<p>The series that follows takes each consequence of the Impression Gap into its specific domain: what happens to engagement when presence is automated, to brand identity when representation is delegated, to content strategy when there are two filters before a human reader, to the trust architecture when the implicit contract of online presence expires without ceremony, and to what you build today so that you have genuine gravity when the transition makes these questions urgent for everyone.</p>
<p>The work starts with a small test in your own audience. Establish which encounters involve people, what those people return for, and whether a proposed offer serves them. Use that evidence to decide which of these models, if any, deserves a larger investment.</p>
</div>
