<div class="essay-section intro">
<p>A platform can recommend your essay to an assistant that summarizes it for a reader. You earned distribution, but the reader may encounter only the summary.</p>
<div class="article-opening-diagram"><Block0 /></div>
<p>The platform chooses what to surface. The assistant chooses what to carry into its answer. A headline that works for the first may do little to preserve the argument through the second.</p>
</div>
<div class="essay-section">
<div class="section-badge">
<div class="section-icon"><Block1 /></div>
<div class="section-meta"> <span class="section-number">Section 01</span> <h2 class="section-title">The Second Filter</h2> </div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">

<p>An assistant can decide what to read, summarize, pass along, or discard. That creates a second editorial problem: what remains of the piece when someone else selects its sentences?</p>
<p>I think the two filters create different pressures: earning attention and surviving compression. That is a way of looking at the problem, not a description of every ranking system. A hook may bring someone to a piece without helping a summary carry its argument. If the claim, evidence, and conclusion are hard to find, there is more room for the intermediary to lose the point.</p>
<div class="pull-quote">
<p>You spent years learning to write for a machine that showed you to people. Now there's another machine that reads you instead of them.</p>
</div>
<p>And the human using that agent doesn't know what was omitted. They got an answer. The gap between your piece and their understanding is invisible to everyone.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="essay-section">
<div class="section-badge">
<div class="section-icon"><Block2 /></div>
<div class="section-meta"> <span class="section-number">Section 02</span> <h2 class="section-title">The User Who Doesn't Know</h2> </div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<p>The human who uses a search engine today is often running their query through an agent without knowing it. Google now answers many questions directly at the top of the page, before any links. An assistant can assemble an answer with source links that the person may never open. These aren't power users with autonomous setups. They're ordinary people doing ordinary searches, and an intermediary is reading on their behalf before the results arrive.</p>
<p>An answer can satisfy a search without bringing a reader to the source. That possibility matters here; it does not establish a traffic trend for every publisher. To know what is happening on a particular site, I would separate human visits, crawler requests, referrals, and completed actions.</p>
<p>The traffic still comes. The reader behind it arrives less often.</p>
<p>That's what makes the optimization instinct so counterproductive. The natural response to declining reach is to optimize harder for the first filter: more SEO, sharper hooks, better headlines. But if the bottleneck has shifted to the second filter, that optimization is solving for the wrong machine. You're getting better at being surfaced by something that routes you to something else that decides you're not worth reading directly.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="essay-section">
<div class="section-badge">
<div class="section-icon"><Block3 /></div>
<div class="section-meta"> <span class="section-number">Section 03</span> <h2 class="section-title">What the Second Filter Reads</h2> </div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<p>A summary can carry the main claim while losing the sequence of examples that made it convincing. That matters when the point depends on how a reader reaches a conclusion, not just on the conclusion itself.</p>
<p>What doesn't compress: vibe, accumulation, the texture of thinking rather than content. The movement from question to observation to something you didn't see coming. That movement is what produces curiosity in a reader, the sense that there's more to find, that the person writing knows something you haven't gotten to yet. The agent reads it, extracts the conclusion, passes along something technically accurate and entirely flat. The essay becomes a bullet point. The argument becomes a sentence. The curiosity doesn't make it through.</p>
<p>If you optimize for both filters at once, you end up writing something compressible and positionally consistent. That's a different kind of writing than the kind worth doing. The filter doesn't just change what gets read. It changes what gets built, if you let it.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="essay-section">
<div class="section-badge">
<div class="section-icon"><Block4 /></div>
<div class="section-meta"> <span class="section-number">Section 04</span> <h2 class="section-title">Digging with the Wrong Tool</h2> </div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<p>The optimization instinct is hard to kill because it used to work, and because the feedback loop that would tell you it stopped working is broken. A total request count does not tell you how many people read a piece. Search impressions and visits answer different questions, and crawler traffic needs separate analysis. The metrics can keep rewarding changes that do not bring more readers to the original piece.</p>
<p>Check the version of the work that actually reaches the reader.</p>
<p>Measurement is catching up. <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports">Google announced dedicated generative AI performance reports in June 2026</a>. Those reports help identify exposure; they still do not tell me whether a reader understood the argument.</p>
<p>The production side compounds this. The content being published is increasingly agentic itself, as argued in the previous essay in this series. An agent creates the piece. Another distributes it. A third, on the reader's side, summarizes it for a human who may or may not have asked for it. At some point in that chain, the question of what any of it means to a person becomes genuinely hard to locate.</p>
<p>A production chain instructed to chase SEO checklists and engagement hooks may reproduce those priorities at every step. That is a risk in how the workflow is configured, not proof of what every model was trained to reward. <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide">Google’s own guidance</a> says there is no special writing style required for its generative search features.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="essay-section">
<div class="section-badge">
<div class="section-icon"><Block5 /></div>
<div class="section-meta"> <span class="section-number">Section 05</span> <h2 class="section-title">The Thing the Filter Can't Flatten</h2> </div>
</div>
<div class="section-rule"></div>
<div class="section-body">
<p>There's a version of this that ends in paralysis: if the filters flatten everything, why write anything? But that logic only holds if surviving the filters is the point.</p>
<p>The reach model made that feel obvious: you write to be found, being found means a human read you, optimize to be found. Distribution and readership were treated as the same thing. They weren't, even before agents. The second filter just made the gap between them impossible to ignore.</p>
<p>What the filter can't flatten is the reason someone seeks the original. A summary can carry a claim. It can't carry the judgment that the person who made the claim is worth trusting. That trust, the sense that a particular voice has a particular relationship to a domain over time, doesn't compress. It accumulates in the person, not in the agent.</p>
<span class="cite">On the structural shift from reach to indexing, and on the synthesis-without-citation problem: "The Last Human Impression" (diegodella.ar, 2026). "The agent may draw on your perspective to form its answer, and the human who receives that answer never knows you exist. There is no traffic. No referral. The economic model for being synthesized rather than cited is still genuinely unresolved."</span>
</div>
</div>
<div class="closing">
<div class="closing-ornament-top">✦ ❧ ✦</div>
<p>I want to write work that gives someone a reason to seek the original.</p>
