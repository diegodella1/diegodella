OriginGravity

Where attention goes when everything else is delegated

On what survives when the intermediated web becomes agent territory, and why the human origin of an idea is the one thing that cannot be delegated.

Diego Dell'Agostino  ·  Buenos Aires

There is something an agent cannot simulate. Not quality. Agents produce quality. Not style, not voice, not consistency, not the ability to hit every structural beat of a form you've trained on for years. All of that can be reproduced, improved upon, made more reliable than any human writing under pressure on a deadline. None of it is the thing.

The thing is origin. The fact that the thought occurred in someone. That the person had something at stake in the question. That the knowledge was earned in conditions that were specific and unrepeatable before it ever became content.

An agent can simulate the output of origin. It cannot be the origin. That asymmetry is the whole answer to the question this series has been carrying since the first essay.

Section 01 What Origin Is

Origin is not biography. You don't have to reveal anything about your life. It is not performed vulnerability or the behind-the-scenes post. Origin is structural: the thinking happened inside a particular mind, in a particular situation, and the situation left a mark on the thinking that cannot be fully extracted from it.

You recognize it as a reader when you find it. Not as a credential. As a pressure. The way a piece weights certain things and not others. What it treats as obvious versus what it treats as worth explaining. The angle of entry into the problem. These are traces of a standpoint, and the standpoint is not in the text. It is in the person. The text is what the standpoint produced, but the two are not the same thing. An agent trained on that text inherits the conclusions. The standpoint stays behind.

Section 02 Why Gravity

Gravity because it operates below the level of decision. You don't choose to feel it. You notice, after the fact, that you went looking for the source. The agent gave you the answer, the answer was correct, and you still opened another tab. You followed someone for two years not because every post was good but because there was a mind on the other end and you wanted to stay near it.

Agents produce answers. Answers close questions. Origin produces orientation: a way of seeing a problem that you can borrow and then keep. The third essay in this series named what the second filter cannot compress: curiosity. Curiosity in the reader is a response to origin in the writer. One mind recognizing another mind that has been somewhere it wants to go. That recognition is not something you can generate by simulating it. It requires that someone was actually there.

An answer satisfies what you asked. Origin changes what you think to ask next. That gap is where the human still lives.

Section 03 What Survives

The agentic internet is very good at moving information and very bad at preserving where the information came from. The agent that summarizes your piece extracts the claim and loses the standpoint. The reader gets the conclusion without the person who produced it.

What origin gravity describes is the behavior on the other side of that loss: the reader who goes around the filters, finds the source directly, subscribes not to the feed but to the person. Not common. Does not need to be common. In a web where agents handle most of the noise, the signal underneath becomes proportionally more valuable.

The empty room this series started with is real. The engagement metrics don't distinguish between a human and a process. But the room is not equally empty everywhere. Some creators still have people in it. Because origin gravity pulled those people through the noise.
Section 04 What It Means to Create Now

Not: be more personal. Not: perform vulnerability. Those are strategies for appearing to have origin, which is a different project with different results. What it means is that the work that matters is work where you were actually somewhere, where the question cost you something, where the thinking left a mark that cannot be lifted from the text and carried away without you.

You cannot schedule that. You cannot A/B test your way into a standpoint. The agentic internet does not make this harder. It makes it more legible. When agents handle the surface competently, the surface stops being a differentiator. What remains is what was always underneath.

Whether you were actually there.

Section 05 The Answer

The first essay said: I think there's something in that relationship that doesn't survive delegation. But we've never had to name it, because we've never had to defend it.

The name is origin.

You cannot delegate origin because origin is not a property of content. It is a property of the person who produced it, and it persists in the work as something readers with real curiosity are looking for. Not the claim. Not the style. The answer to a question a summary cannot answer: was there someone in here? Is there a mind I can follow somewhere the answer didn't go?

The clicks are still coming. Agents are generating behavior at every layer of the system. But underneath that behavior, in the part of the audience that cannot be automated, there is still a pull toward human origin that no agent produces by simulating it. Because the pull is not toward the content.

It is toward the person the content proves existed.
